# Endorsement disclosure notes (US)

Summary for reviewers — not legal advice. Primary sources: FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023) and the FTC "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers" guidance; FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, effective 2024).

- A **material connection** (payment, free product, discount, family/employee relationship, affiliate commission) between an endorser and the brand must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously.
- Disclosure should be hard to miss: at the start of a caption, not buried in hashtags or behind "more"; spoken *and* on-screen in video.
- Acceptable plain-language forms: "Ad", "#ad", "Sponsored", "Paid partnership with…", "Thanks to [Brand] for the free product". Platform branded-content tools may be used but don't automatically satisfy the requirement.
- **Fake reviews/testimonials** — writing reviews attributed to people who don't exist or didn't have the experience, or misrepresenting reviews — are prohibited by the 2024 Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, which allows civil penalties.
- Testimonials claiming results must reflect what consumers can generally expect, or clearly state typical results.

Have counsel review policies for regulated industries (health, finance, legal, alcohol, cannabis, firearms).
